What this is not

Not an official warning system — the Department of Hydrology and Meteorology issues Nepal's warnings, and this does not. Not a dispatch system: it has no authority to move anyone or anything, and does not pretend to. Not a replacement for BIPAD — it is a lens on it, read-only.

It does one thing: it turns a public record that no member of the public can read into one that answers questions, and it ends every answer with a verb.

Data sources

  • Disaster incidents, river gauges, road closures and facilities: BIPAD Portal, National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Authority (NDRRMA), Government of Nepal. No licence stated — shown for informational purposes only, not redistributed.
  • River and rainfall observations originate from the Department of Hydrology and Meteorology (DHM), Government of Nepal. No licence stated.
  • Road closure reports originate from the Department of Roads (DoR), Government of Nepal. No licence stated.
  • Global disaster alerts: GDACS — a cooperation framework of the European Commission, UN OCHA and UNOSAT. Indicative only.
  • River discharge forecasts: Open-Meteo.com (CC BY 4.0). Contains modified Copernicus Emergency Management Service information 2026.
  • Map data: © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0.
  • Basemap and satellite tiles: Esri — Esri, HERE, Garmin, Maxar, Earthstar Geographics, © OpenStreetMap contributors and the GIS User Community.
  • Mapping campaign coordinated by the Humanitarian OpenStreetMap Team (HOT) with NAXA and NDRRMA.
  • Built with Leaflet (BSD-2-Clause).

What the research found missing

  • No public self-service flood-warning subscription for citizens.
  • No confirmed public web form to report an incident into BIPAD.
  • No aggregation endpoint — Nepal's own disaster data cannot be summarised without scraping it yourself. Every total on this page is computed in your browser.
  • No published licence or documentation on an API that serves national disaster data.
  • Unskilled international volunteers are not wanted on the ground. Accredited medical, WASH, structural, search-and-rescue and geospatial specialists are. Mapping and cash are what everyone else can usefully give.
